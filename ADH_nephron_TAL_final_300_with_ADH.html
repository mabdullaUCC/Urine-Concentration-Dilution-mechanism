Water exits the descending limb regardless of ADH. The ascending limb reabsorbs NaCl without water. ADH regulates collecting-duct water permeability.

Schematic teaching model. Five of ten green urea particles illustrate approximately 50% reabsorption of the filtered urea load in the proximal tubule (not a 50% fall in tubular urea concentration). Removing urea represents reduced medullary urea recycling (not complete elimination of urea from the body); the 750 mOsm/kg ceiling is an illustrative teaching assumption, not a fixed physiological value. Urine osmolality is illustrative, not a prediction for an individual patient. SIADH is represented by persistently high ADH action, not a specific diagnostic urine osmolality; actual values depend on water and solute intake. Select central DI to demonstrate correction by added ADH, or nephrogenic DI to demonstrate ADH resistance. For the hypothetical TAL mutation, ~150 mOsm/kg (minimum) and ~300 mOsm/kg (maximum) are illustrative assumptions, NOT established clinical values. The water-impermeable distal convoluted tubule continues to reabsorb NaCl and dilute tubular fluid. ADH can still affect collecting-duct water permeability, although medullary concentrating capacity is reduced. The actual result would depend on residual transport, permeability, urea recycling and other nephron segments. Values are illustrative.
